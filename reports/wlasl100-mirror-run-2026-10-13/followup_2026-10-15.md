# Offline follow-up — 2026-10-15

The user reported that an informal webcam trial recognized “eat” but got most other attempts wrong. The signs attempted, number of trials, tracking quality, and FPS were not logged, so this is feedback rather than a live accuracy measurement. No camera was accessed in this follow-up.

## Coverage and validation-only model check

Of 2,038 expected WLASL100 clips, 1,013 have caches and 1,025 are missing: 694 train, 173 validation, and 158 test. There are a median of 7 available training clips and 2 validation clips per class; 11 classes have no validation clip and 28 have no test clip. A recursive search of the local video folders found no missing WLASL100 IDs under another folder or video extension.

The [feature experiment](validation_feature_experiment.json) trained logistic regression on the existing **train** caches and compared settings on the 165 available **validation** clips only. It did not read test examples. All variants used the same split, scaling, class weighting, and seed. The saved baseline remains the best of these comparisons.

| Landmark summary | C=1 validation top-1 | C=10 validation top-1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean over time | 0.4061 | **0.4121** |
| Mean + standard deviation | 0.4061 | 0.4000 |
| Mean + standard deviation + start/end difference | 0.3333 | 0.3091 |
| Four time-bin means | 0.3636 | 0.3636 |

The C=1 mean result differs by one validation clip from the earlier baseline-selection run; the selected C=10 score matches. No new model was selected or evaluated on test.

## Exploratory hand-tracking diagnosis

In the **already evaluated** 100 test clips, the baseline got 3/34 clips correct when an assigned hand appeared in fewer than half the frames, versus 29/66 of the other clips. The GRU got 9/34 versus 21/66. These are retrospective subgroup counts, not a causal test or a new model-selection criterion. The groups may differ in sign, signer, and video quality.

The [six-clip detector check](hand_assignment_diagnostic.json) compared raw MediaPipe hand detections with the cached anatomically assigned hand slots. For “give” (`24636`), raw hands appeared in 77.2% of frames but assigned hands in only 5.4%; usable pose wrists appeared in 22.8%. This points to wrist-based assignment dropping many detected hands in that clip. For “dog” (`65506`) and “woman” (`63664`), raw detector coverage itself was low (32.0% and 28.1%), so assignment changes alone cannot recover most frames. The other three sampled clips lost 1.0–2.1 percentage points between raw detection and assignment. Six selected clips do not establish how common either failure mode is across the dataset.

## Next work

1. Obtain and verify more pre-trimmed WLASL clips through the official access workflow, especially missing training and validation examples. Preserve official splits and use a new manifest/cache for any new extraction.
2. Investigate hand detection and wrist assignment on a larger, representative sample before changing the extractor. Re-extracting would require retraining and reevaluation; old caches and new caches must not be mixed.
3. Only after a model is selected using validation, run a documented live trial with known ASL signs, hand-tracking observations, and FPS. The current offline scores do not establish live usability.
